A debt you'll be glad you paid

Settled doesn't ban your apps. It makes a deal with you — going over is fine, as long as you pay the time back.

1

Decide how much is enough

Pick how long you're happy to spend on social media in one go — 15, 30 or 45 minutes. Your number, your choice.

2

Go over, and the apps close

When your time runs out, a calm screen appears over Instagram, TikTok — whichever apps you chose — and they stay closed until you've paid the extra minutes back.

3

Pay it back with real life

Owe 30 minutes? Read for 30 minutes. Or walk, run, cook, tidy, meditate. Walks and runs are checked against your phone's own step counter.

4

Watch it add up

Every minute you pay back is saved into one number: time invested in yourself. Minutes become hours. It only ever grows.

A week you can hold

Place activities on the days you want them. Sessions tick themselves off — pay a debt with a walk and Wednesday's walk lights up on its own.

  • Goals, planned by day. “Exercise Mon, Wed, Fri” — not a vague weekly wish.
  • One-line proofs become a journal. “What did you read about?” Your answers quietly build a written record of your weeks.
  • Milestones worth reaching. 10 hours. 100. A thousand. And at ten thousand — mastery.
  • Every Monday, a fresh start. Weeks close, get their number, and stack into history.

Quiet by design

Most apps in this category nag. Settled speaks at most twice a day, only in the evening, and only ever to help or congratulate.

  • No streak guilt. Slipping doesn't break anything — going to bed in debt does, and even that just resets a number.
  • No “you failed” notifications. The weekly result only arrives when it's good news.
  • No leaderboards, no sharing, no comparison. The only person you answer to is the one who set the alarm.

Private by architecture

Not a promise — a design. There is nothing to leak, because nothing leaves.

  • Collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Settled has no servers.
  • Everything stays on your phone. Your usage, goals, notes and history live in the app's private storage — and die with an uninstall.
  • Never uses your camera. Proof is a written line, not a photograph of your home.
